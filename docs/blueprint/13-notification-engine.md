13 - Notification Engine

Purpose

The Notification Engine creates and manages communication requests generated by platform events.

It never sends messages directly.

All delivery is asynchronous through a Notification Queue and delivery workers.

Supported channels in MVP:

* SMS
* Viber
* Mobile Push Notifications
* In-App Notifications

⸻

Responsibilities

The Notification Engine is responsible for:

* receiving Notification Events
* selecting notification templates
* selecting allowed channels
* checking customer preferences and consent
* creating Notification Jobs
* preventing duplicate notifications
* tracking notification status

The Notification Engine never:

* sends messages directly
* calculates Reward Points
* calculates XP
* changes Membership Status
* executes loyalty business logic

⸻

Notification Flow

Business Event
↓
Automation Rule
↓
Notification Engine
↓
Notification Job
↓
Notification Queue
↓
Delivery Worker
↓
SMS / Viber / Push / In-App

The original business workflow does not wait for message delivery.

⸻

Notification Channels

SMS

Used for:

* OTP
* important transactional messages
* fallback when Viber is unavailable

⸻

Viber

Used for:

* transactional notifications
* loyalty updates
* promotions
* birthday and welcome messages

⸻

Mobile Push

Used for:

* points earned
* status changed
* challenge progress
* points expiring
* available rewards
* promotional notifications

Push notifications require an active registered device.

⸻

In-App Notifications

Stored and displayed inside the Customer App.

Examples:

* You earned 50 points.
* Your points become available in 15 days.
* You reached Gold Status.
* 100 points expire in 7 days.

In-App notifications remain visible until read or archived.

⸻

Channel Selection

Notification templates define allowed channels.

Example:

OTP:
SMS or Viber
Points Earned:
Push + In-App
Status Upgraded:
Push + In-App + optional Viber
Marketing Promotion:
Push, Viber or SMS according to consent

The system may use channel priority and fallback.

Example:

Viber
↓ unavailable
SMS

Push and In-App may be sent together.

⸻

Transactional and Marketing Notifications

Transactional

Required for platform operation.

Examples:

* OTP
* points earned
* points redeemed
* receipt reversed
* status changed
* points expiring

Transactional notifications are controlled by legal and platform rules.

⸻

Marketing

Examples:

* promotions
* double points campaign
* happy hour
* win-back message
* special offers

Marketing messages require explicit user consent.

⸻

Notification Preferences

Customers may configure:

* SMS enabled
* Viber enabled
* Push enabled
* Marketing consent

In-App transactional notifications remain available inside the app.

Mandatory security messages cannot be disabled.

⸻

Notification Templates

Every template contains:

* template type
* language
* channel
* title
* message body
* variables
* transactional or marketing classification
* active status

Example variables:

{{customer_name}}
{{business_name}}
{{points}}
{{status_name}}
{{expiration_date}}

⸻

Notification Job

Every job stores:

* recipient
* business
* membership
* channel
* template
* payload
* scheduled time
* priority
* status
* attempt count
* idempotency key

Duplicate suppression must be deterministic across retries and provider failures.

Provider retries must never create duplicate customer-visible notifications.

Statuses:

* queued
* processing
* sent
* delivered
* failed
* cancelled

⸻

Queue Processing

Delivery Workers process queued notifications asynchronously.

Workers are responsible for:

* provider communication
* retries
* timeout handling
* provider response storage
* delivery status updates

⸻

Retry Policy

Retries are allowed only for temporary delivery failures.

Examples:

* provider timeout
* temporary network error
* rate limit
* temporary provider outage

Permanent failures are not retried indefinitely.

Examples:

* invalid phone number
* disabled device token
* missing consent
* unsupported channel

⸻

Duplicate Protection

The same business event must not create the same notification more than once.

Duplicate protection uses:

* source event
* recipient
* template type
* channel
* idempotency key

⸻

Push Devices

The platform stores registered mobile devices.

Each device contains:

* user
* platform
* push token
* app version
* last active time
* status

Platforms:

* Android
* iOS

Invalid push tokens must be disabled automatically.

⸻

In-App Notification Inbox

Customer App displays:

* unread notifications
* notification history
* created time
* related business
* related membership
* optional deep link

A notification may open a related screen.

Examples:

* Reward Points
* Status Progress
* Challenge
* Business Profile

⸻

Scheduling

Notification Jobs may be:

* immediate
* delayed
* scheduled

Examples:

* points earned: immediate
* birthday message: scheduled
* points expiration reminder: 30, 7 and 1 day before expiration

⸻

Security and Privacy

Notification content must not expose sensitive information unnecessarily.

OTP codes must never be stored in plain text.

Phone numbers and provider credentials must be protected.

One Business may never send messages to customers outside its permitted memberships.

⸻

Monitoring

The system tracks:

* queued messages
* sent messages
* delivered messages
* failed messages
* retry count
* channel cost
* provider latency
* invalid recipients

⸻

Future Extensions

Future channels may include:

* Email
* WhatsApp
* Web Push
* Apple Wallet notifications
* Google Wallet notifications

New channels must be added without changing business engines.

⸻

Design Principles

Business Engines publish Events.

The Notification Engine creates Notification Jobs.

Delivery Workers send messages.

Message delivery must never block the original business transaction.
