45 - Notification Matrix

Purpose

This document defines which platform events generate notifications, who receives them, which channels are used and whether delivery is mandatory, optional or marketing-related.

The Notification Matrix is the business source of truth for communication behavior.

The Notification Engine and delivery workers must follow this document.

⸻

Notification Principles

* Business transactions must not wait for notification delivery.
* Notifications are created asynchronously.
* Transactional and marketing notifications are treated differently.
* Marketing communication requires valid consent.
* Security-critical communication cannot be disabled.
* In-App notifications are preferred for routine loyalty activity.
* SMS should be reserved for OTP, important transactional messages and fallback.
* Businesses must not send notifications to Customers outside permitted Membership relationships.
* The same event must not create duplicate notifications.

⸻

Supported Channels

SMS

Primary uses:

* OTP
* security alerts
* important transactional fallback

SMS has direct delivery cost and should not be used unnecessarily.

⸻

Viber

Primary uses:

* OTP when supported
* important transactional communication
* selected marketing campaigns
* win-back communication

SMS may be used as fallback where configured.

⸻

Mobile Push

Primary uses:

* Reward activity
* Status changes
* Challenges
* Instant Rewards
* expiring Rewards
* promotions

Requires an active registered device.

⸻

In-App

Primary uses:

* loyalty history
* progress updates
* available Benefits
* Challenges
* Reward Opportunities
* program announcements

In-App notifications remain available in the Customer notification inbox.

⸻

Notification Classification

Security

Required for identity and account protection.

Examples:

* OTP
* suspicious login
* account anonymization confirmation

Security notifications cannot be disabled.

⸻

Transactional

Generated by a Customer or Business transaction.

Examples:

* purchase processed
* points earned
* redemption completed
* receipt cancelled

Transactional messages do not require marketing consent, but unnecessary channels may be disabled according to Customer preferences.

⸻

Engagement

Generated to support active participation.

Examples:

* Challenge progress
* Reward Goal progress
* Instant Reward available
* points expiring

Engagement notifications follow Customer channel preferences.

⸻

Marketing

Promotional communication intended to influence future behavior.

Examples:

* Happy Hour
* Double Points
* seasonal promotion
* win-back offer

Marketing consent is mandatory.

⸻

Channel Priority

Recommended default priority:

Security:
Viber OTP → SMS fallback
Transactional:
Push + In-App
Important Transactional:
Push + In-App + optional Viber
Marketing:
Push or Viber according to consent and Business configuration

A Notification Template may define a different approved priority.

⸻

Frequency Control

The Notification Engine must prevent excessive messaging.

Recommended controls:

* maximum marketing messages per Customer per day;
* maximum marketing messages per Business per week;
* quiet hours;
* duplicate suppression;
* progress milestone grouping;
* channel preference enforcement.

Security and essential transactional messages are exempt from marketing frequency limits.

⸻

Quiet Hours

Businesses may configure quiet hours for non-critical notifications.

Recommended default:

21:00–08:00 local Customer time

Notifications scheduled during quiet hours are delayed unless classified as:

* Security
* Critical Transactional
* Time-sensitive OTP

⸻

Localization

Every Notification Job must resolve:

* Customer preferred language;
* Brand language fallback;
* Platform language fallback;
* Customer time zone;
* Brand naming and terminology.

Templates must not contain hardcoded business names, currencies or date formats.

⸻

Consent Rules

Security

Consent not required.

Transactional

Marketing consent not required.

Customer preferences may control non-essential channels.

Engagement

Customer notification preferences apply.

Marketing

Explicit valid consent required.

Consent withdrawal must take effect before new marketing Notification Jobs are created.

⸻

Duplicate Protection

Notification uniqueness should consider:

* source Event;
* Customer;
* Membership;
* template;
* channel;
* campaign;
* scheduled occurrence.

Retries must update the existing Notification Job rather than create a new one.

Notification delivery retries must be idempotent.

Repeated provider retries must never create duplicate customer-visible notifications.

⸻

Notification Audit

Store:

* source Event;
* recipient;
* Business and Brand;
* Membership;
* classification;
* channel;
* template version;
* consent state;
* scheduling decision;
* delivery attempts;
* final status;
* provider identifier.

Message content may be retained according to privacy and data-retention rules.

⸻

Failure Handling

Temporary failures:

* retry with backoff;
* use configured fallback channel where allowed.

Permanent failures:

* disable invalid device token;
* mark invalid phone destination;
* stop retries;
* create operational alert where necessary.

Failure to send a notification must never reverse the originating business transaction.

⸻

Testing Requirements

Mandatory scenarios:

* OTP uses Viber with SMS fallback.
* Marketing message rejected without consent.
* Transactional message sent without marketing consent.
* Disabled Push preference respected.
* Security message cannot be disabled.
* Duplicate Event does not create duplicate Notification.
* Quiet hours delay marketing notification.
* Expiration reminders sent at configured intervals.
* Instant Reward result not exposed before opening.
* Cross-tenant notification attempt rejected.
* Invalid Push token disabled.
* Temporary provider failure retried.
* Permanent destination failure not retried indefinitely.

⸻

Design Principle

Notifications should strengthen the Customer relationship without creating noise.

The correct message must reach the correct recipient, through an allowed channel, at an appropriate time, for a clear business reason.
