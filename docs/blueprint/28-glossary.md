Purpose

This glossary defines the official terminology used throughout the platform.

All Blueprint documents, implementations, APIs and AI agents should use these definitions consistently.

Each business concept has exactly one meaning.

⸻

Business

A company using the platform to operate one or more Loyalty Programs.

A Business may own multiple Locations.

⸻

Location

A physical place where customers earn or redeem Reward Points.

Examples:

* Coffee Shop
* Restaurant
* Store
* Playground

A Location always belongs to one Business.

⸻

Customer

A person participating in one or more Loyalty Programs.

A Customer may hold multiple Memberships.

⸻

Membership

The relationship between a Customer and a Loyalty Program.

Membership contains:

* Reward Account
* XP Progress
* Membership Status
* Benefits

Membership is the primary business entity representing participation.

⸻

Loyalty Program

The business configuration defining how customers earn, redeem and progress.

Examples:

* Reward Rules
* XP Rules
* Status Levels
* Benefits
* Point Expiration

Each Business may own one or more Loyalty Programs.

⸻

Reward Points

Reward Points represent monetary value granted to a Customer.

Reward Points may be redeemed according to business rules.

Reward Points are tracked through immutable Reward Transactions.

⸻

Reward Account

The business representation of a Customer’s Reward Point balance.

The balance is calculated from Reward Transactions.

Reward Accounts never store business history directly.

⸻

Reward Transaction

An immutable record describing a change in Reward Points.

Examples:

* Earn
* Release
* Reserve
* Redeem
* Reverse
* Expire
* Adjustment

Reward Transactions are append-only.

⸻

XP

Experience Points measure customer engagement.

XP has no monetary value.

XP cannot be redeemed or transferred.

XP contributes to Membership progression.

⸻

XP Transaction

An immutable record describing a change in XP.

Examples:

* Purchase
* Visit
* Challenge
* Promotion
* Welcome Bonus
* Birthday Bonus
* Adjustment

⸻

Membership Status

Represents the current loyalty level of a Membership.

Examples:

* Bronze
* Silver
* Gold
* Platinum

Status determines available Benefits.

⸻

Benefit

A privilege granted to a Membership.

Examples:

* Reward multiplier
* Extended expiration
* Exclusive promotion
* Priority rewards

Benefits may originate from different business mechanisms.

⸻

Visit

A qualifying customer interaction recognized by the Loyalty Program.

A Visit is independent of Reward Points.

Visits contribute to customer progression.

⸻

Receipt

A business transaction processed by the platform.

A Receipt may generate:

* Reward Points
* XP
* Events
* Notifications

Receipts are immutable after processing.

⸻

Automation

A business rule that reacts to one or more Events.

Automations execute predefined business actions.

⸻

Event

An immutable fact describing something that happened.

Examples:

* Receipt Created
* Reward Earned
* XP Earned
* Status Changed

Events coordinate business workflows.

⸻

Automation Engine

Coordinates business workflows.

The Automation Engine executes business rules.

It delegates calculations to Business Engines.

⸻

Reward Engine

Responsible for the complete lifecycle of Reward Points.

⸻

XP Engine

Responsible for XP calculations and engagement tracking.

⸻

Status Engine

Responsible for Membership progression and Status evaluation.

⸻

Notification Engine

Creates Notification Jobs based on business events.

Message delivery is performed asynchronously.

⸻

Notification Worker

Processes Notification Jobs and communicates with delivery providers.

Workers never contain business logic.

⸻

Loyalty Network

A cooperation agreement between Loyalty Programs allowing cross-program Reward Point redemption.

Reward ownership never changes.

⸻

Settlement

The financial reconciliation process between participating Businesses.

Settlement does not modify Reward Transactions.

⸻

Business Event

A domain event generated by a completed business operation.

Business Events are immutable.

⸻

Projection

A read model derived from immutable business history.

Examples:

* Reward Balance
* Current XP
* Current Status

Projections improve performance.

They never replace source data.

⸻

Ledger

The immutable transaction history representing the source of truth.

Examples:

Reward Ledger

XP Ledger

⸻

Audit Record

An immutable record describing administrative or security-sensitive actions.

Audit Records support traceability.

⸻

Blueprint

The complete product architecture documentation.

The Blueprint is the primary source of truth for platform design.

⸻

Skill

A reusable, narrowly focused capability available to AI agents.

Skills contain specialized guidance rather than project-specific knowledge.

⸻

Project Knowledge

Project-specific information that supplements the Blueprint.

Examples:

* naming conventions
* infrastructure
* deployment strategy
* operational decisions

⸻

AI Agent

An engineering collaborator operating according to the Blueprint, Skills and Project Knowledge.

AI agents do not redefine business rules.

⸻

Source of Truth

The authoritative definition of a business concept.

Business Rules take precedence over implementation.

Blueprint documents define the platform.

Implementation follows the Blueprint.

⸻

Glossary Maintenance

Every new business concept introduced into the platform should be added to this Glossary before implementation.

Terminology consistency is considered an architectural requirement.
